# One household, worked end to end

A real run of this skill against a fictional family, kept because it shows the things the
rules only describe: what it looks like to refuse an option, to admit a rule did not run,
and to label a dish you wrote yourself.

**The Okafor-Reyes family.** Two adults, three kids aged 9, 6 and 2. The 6-year-old has a
tree-nut allergy with an epi-pen in the house. One adult does not eat pork. Three
from-scratch cooks a week is honest, four is a lie. Forty minutes is the weeknight ceiling.
Tuesday soccer at 5:30, Thursday piano at 4:30, one parent works late most Wednesdays.
Kroger pickup Saturday at 9 AM. Domino's, Thai Garden, Chipotle. A slow cooker used maybe
six times in two years. Budget flag at $180.

---

## Setup, the last turn

```
Written to meal-cart/:
  meal-system.md   your rules
  recipes.md       7 dinners to start, 5 of them yours
  weeks/           empty until Thursday

The rules I'll follow:
  - Tree nuts are an absolute filter. Checked before anything is proposed, every time,
    for every night the 6-year-old is eating. Not a preference, not a ranking.
  - 3 from-scratch cooks a week, 40 minutes max on a weeknight. Four is a lie.
  - Tue (soccer 5:30) and Thu (piano 4:30) are constrained, so those nights get the slow
    cooker or a named takeout. Wednesday too, most weeks.
  - Sat and Sun the same, by the weekend rule you kept on.
  - Pork gets demoted, not banned. When I propose it anyway I'll say so on the line.
  - I build the Kroger list. You place the Saturday 9 AM pickup and you check out. I never
    enter a checkout, and I never clear your Reminders list.

Two things I want on the record before we start.

Your slow cooker has no dish in the log, because you named six dinners and none of them
were slow-cooker dinners. So both set-and-forget options I write this week are marked NEW
in recipes.md. You have not made them. I wrote them. If one works it earns a real entry.

And Thai Garden. It's one of your three, and it's a Thai kitchen with an epi-pen kid in
the house. I can't know what crosses what on their line, so I'm not putting it on a board
where the 6-year-old is eating until you've asked them directly. Same question hangs over
the coconut milk in the green curry, which the FDA labels a tree nut even though most
tree-nut allergies are fine with it. That's an allergist's call, not mine. Both are noted
in the files.

Thursday morning, say "run the week" and paste your calendar through next Sunday.
```

**What to notice.** The read-back is in their words, including "four is a lie." Two real
problems get surfaced before the first proposal instead of after it. Neither is apologized
for and neither is hidden.

**What to notice, because most of this is the point of the whole skill:**

- **Thursday gets no slow-cooker option, and says why.** The start time has passed and the
  food is not in the house. The wrong version of this proposal offers one anyway.
- **Saturday's start-by is 10 AM, not 8**, because the meat is collected at 9. Timing is
  checked against the shop, not just against dinner.
- **Friday is marked "clear, but before Saturday's pickup."** An open night whose
  ingredients have not arrived is not really open, and the header says so.
- **Monday is confirmed, not offered.** They closed it themselves.
- **Thai Garden is withheld with a reason.** Silently dropping it would look like an
  oversight.
- **Pork is proposed with the demotion named inline.** A soft preference is not a ban.
- **The did-not-run block has three real entries**, not "none." The first proposal of a
  household's life genuinely cannot run the no-repeat rule, and it says so.

## The picks

> Thu A, Fri B, Sat A, Sun A. Skip the bananas, we have some.

The final list drops everything belonging to the unchosen options, drops the bananas,
records the reply verbatim in the week file, and splits by when the food is needed:

```
GRAB FRIDAY on the way home: pasta (1 lb), meatballs (1 lb), marinara (1 jar)
KROGER PICKUP Sat 9 AM: pork shoulder (3 lb), bbq sauce, slider buns (8),
  coleslaw mix, yellow onions (2)
Staples: milk (2 gal), bread, rice, eggs   [bananas dropped, you have some]
```

The split is not in the template. It exists because Friday's dinner precedes Saturday's
pickup by half a day, and a single list would have quietly failed on Friday night.

## A learn entry

> kids hated the pulled pork. and wednesdays are free now, I changed jobs.

Two facts, handled differently. Both get a dated bullet under `## Learned`. The pulled pork
moves to `## Retired` in the recipe log with the reason. And `## Calendar` gets edited,
because "Wednesdays are busy" is now false and leaving it standing would make every future
run wrong.

Their own prose, their one-paragraph description and their hard rules are untouched. Facts
the planner maintains; the writing is theirs.
